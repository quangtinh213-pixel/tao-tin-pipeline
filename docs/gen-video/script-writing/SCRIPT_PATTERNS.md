# SCRIPT_PATTERNS

This file picks the **narrative arc** for `plan.json` segments. Use it after
`SCRIPT_RULES.md`, before borrowing a style from `STYLE_INDEX.md`.

Style answers: what voice does this speak in.
Pattern answers: what path makes the viewer understand and want to keep
watching.

Patterns are chosen by **story shape**, not by news category. A `tech-dark`
video about a product launch and a `business-ledger-light` video about a
company launch both use the Product/Update pattern below; only the visuals
and vocabulary differ by category.

## 1. Default arc

- Hook: one sentence, the single most watch-worthy fact.
- First body segment: proof, concrete fact, figure, or the first strong
  visual beat. Do not make the viewer wait long for evidence.
- Middle body segments: mechanism, examples, figures, risk, or how to read
  the situation.
- Ending: a takeaway, a caution, or a perspective to remember. Never close on
  an empty slogan.

## 2. Breaking / current-affairs news

Use for `current-affairs-*`, `real-estate-civic-*`, `health-public-alert`,
and any fast-moving event story.

- Hook: what happened, to whom or where, stated plainly. No speculation
  framed as fact. Do not repeat the headline; lead with the detail or
  consequence it omits (see `SCRIPT_RULES.md` §2).
- Segment 1: the confirmed core fact or figure, sourced.
- Segment 2: why this is happening now / immediate cause.
- Segment 3: who is affected and how.
- Segment 4 (if needed): official response or what happens next.
- Ending: what to watch for next, or a grounded caution. Never end on
  unverified claims presented as settled.

Use hedged legal/factual language for anything not yet confirmed by
authorities: "theo thông tin ban đầu", "cơ quan chức năng đang xác minh". Do
not state disputed facts, allegations, or unconfirmed casualty/damage figures
as settled truth.

## 3. Feature / explainer

Use for `tech-*`, `business-*`, `education-*`, `auto-*`, `beauty-*` pieces
that explain a trend, product category, or how something works.

- Hook: the surprising result, comparison, or gap versus expectation.
- Segment 1: the evidence, demo, figure, or example that proves the hook.
- Segment 2: what is actually being measured/discussed and why it is
  credible or worth questioning.
- Segment 3: the mechanism or trade-off behind that result.
- Segment 4: what to watch out for (data, conditions, hidden cost, fine
  print).
- Ending: the practical takeaway — what to do with this right now.

If several names or figures compete for attention, keep the hook to only the
contrast that matters; move the rest of the explanation later.

## 4. Product / update / launch

Use for a new product, service, policy, price change, or feature rollout in
any category (`business-*`, `auto-*`, `tech-*`, `travel-*`, `entertainment-*`
premieres).

- Hook: what just changed and why it matters to the viewer.
- Segment 1: proof or demonstration of the change.
- Segment 2: how it used to work.
- Segment 3: what is different or unlocked now.
- Segment 4: real conditions to use it — price, availability, region,
  eligibility, or timing.
- Ending: who should act now, who should wait.

Do not let the script read like a press release changelog. Always translate
the update into a consequence for the viewer.

## 5. Warning / alert / risk

Use for `health-public-alert`, safety recalls, scams, weather, or market risk
stories.

- Hook: the risk stated plainly, who it affects.
- Segment 1: the concrete evidence or incident behind the warning.
- Segment 2: why it is happening / the mechanism of the risk.
- Segment 3: who is most exposed and the real (not exaggerated) severity.
- Segment 4: what to do about it, concretely.
- Ending: a clear, calm call to caution. Do not manufacture panic or
  overstate severity beyond the source.

For anything involving money, fees, or promotions, always state the actual
mechanism (who pays, when it expires, what happens after) so the viewer is
never misled into thinking something is free or risk-free when it is not.

## 6. Human-interest / entertainment / sports

Use for `entertainment-*`, `sports-*`, `travel-*` human-focused stories.

- Hook: the person/team/place and the moment that makes this worth watching.
- Segment 1: the moment or result itself, stated vividly.
- Segment 2: context that makes it matter (stakes, history, rivalry, rarity).
- Segment 3: reaction or consequence.
- Ending: what it means going forward, or a memorable closing image. Keep it
  warm, not saccharine.

## 7. Good hooks and bad hooks

Good:

- `Giá vé máy bay nội địa vừa tăng thêm 15% chỉ trong một tuần.`
- `Một cơn bão mới đang hình thành ngoài khơi và có thể đổi hướng bất ngờ.`
- `Ngân hàng này vừa công bố mức lãi suất tiết kiệm cao nhất trong ba năm.`

Bad:

- `Trong video này mình sẽ nói về một tin tức đáng chú ý...`
- `Bài viết đang chia sẻ một câu chuyện khá thú vị về...`
- `Dành cho ai chưa biết thì hôm nay có một sự kiện...`
- `Đây là một chủ đề khá thú vị nên mình làm video này...`
- `Một nam sinh bệnh tim vừa được CSGT mở đường tới viện cấp cứu kịp thời.`
  (reveals the outcome; 29% view percentage)
- `Cảnh báo y tế khẩn: TP.HCM vừa báo động số ca tay chân miệng tăng 65%.`
  (hype label; 24%)
- `Chuỗi phòng tập X vừa phát đi thông báo chính thức về kế hoạch mở cửa lại.`
  (announcement with no viewer stake; 22%)

News hook data and the enforced rules: `SCRIPT_RULES.md` §2.1.

Fix a bad hook by removing the meta layer, putting the real subject at the
front of the sentence, then attaching the concrete result or tension
immediately.
